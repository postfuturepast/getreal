What is stamp duty?

A tax on buying property

Stamp duty is a one-off state government tax paid when you purchase real estate. It's calculated on the purchase price (or market value, whichever is higher) and must be paid at or before settlement — typically within 30 days of signing the contract.

It's one of the largest upfront costs of buying property and is not included in your mortgage. You need to have this money available as part of your deposit.

Why does it vary by state?

Each state and territory sets its own legislation, rates, and concessions. There's no national standard. NSW and VIC are two of the most expensive. The ACT has been transitioning away from stamp duty toward an annual land tax since 2012. NT uses a formula-based calculation rather than a bracket table.

First home buyer concessions

Most states offer stamp duty discounts or exemptions for first home buyers, usually subject to price caps and residency requirements. These can be worth tens of thousands of dollars — but the thresholds are often lower than current median prices in capital cities. See your state's guide for the exact rules.
